**TL;DR (Direct Answer):** The Musk vs. Altman trial in Oakland has turned into a brutal airing of Silicon Valley’s dirtiest laundry. On May 5, 2026, OpenAI President Greg Brockman concluded his testimony by detonating a massive narrative bomb: he revealed that in 2017, Elon Musk secretly enlisted several OpenAI employees to do months of free work for Tesla. The work wasn't for charity; it was specifically aimed at overhauling Tesla's struggling Autopilot self-driving technology. Brockman's testimony aims to shatter Musk’s argument that he was purely a selfless benefactor trying to save humanity, portraying him instead as a billionaire who treated a tax-exempt non-profit like an unpaid R&D wing for his car company.

## The "Free Labor" Bombshell

For the first week of the trial, Elon Musk painted himself as the victim—a visionary who poured tens of millions into OpenAI to protect humanity, only to have Sam Altman steal the charity and sell it to Microsoft. 

Greg Brockman’s time on the stand was specifically designed to dismantle that image. 

Brockman testified under oath that in 2017, while Musk was still funding the organization and sitting on its board of directors, Musk quietly diverted OpenAI's elite engineering talent. Several OpenAI employees were allegedly instructed to work for free on Tesla's proprietary self-driving technology. By framing this as a "secret project" that benefited a for-profit public company (Tesla), OpenAI's lawyers are heavily implying that Musk's real interest in the AI lab was leveraging cheap genius to solve his autonomous driving crisis.

## The Karpathy "Confession"

The poaching of top-tier talent has become a central battleground in the trial. 

Last week, Musk testified that Andrej Karpathy—a legendary AI researcher who left OpenAI to become Tesla’s Director of AI—only joined Tesla after he had already decided to leave the startup. Musk claimed it was a standard career move.

Brockman’s testimony directly contradicted this. He recounted that after Musk hired Karpathy, the billionaire approached Brockman with what Brockman explicitly described as an "apology and a confession." According to Brockman, neither Musk nor Karpathy had informed OpenAI leadership of the planned departure, making it a stealthy extraction of one of the lab's most valuable minds. 

## The Mars Connection and the $50 Billion Bill

Why did Musk want absolute control over OpenAI? According to Brockman, it wasn't just about AI safety. 

Brockman testified that Musk was intensely focused on his ultimate goal: building a city on Mars. In 2017, Musk reportedly calculated that colonizing Mars would cost roughly $80 billion. He saw OpenAI not just as a research lab, but as a potential cash engine. Brockman stated that Musk insisted on converting OpenAI to a for-profit model, but *only* if he was given total control, allowing him to theoretically funnel the massive future profits toward SpaceX's Martian ambitions. 

However, the trial also revealed why OpenAI desperately needed money in the first place. In a jaw-dropping admission, Brockman casually disclosed that OpenAI's computing costs have skyrocketed from $30 million in 2017 to a projected **$50 billion** in 2026. This staggering figure reinforces the defense's core argument: a pure 501(c)(3) charity cannot raise $50 billion to buy server space. Going commercial wasn't greed; it was survival.

## The Verdict Approaches

As the trial rolls into its next phase, the jury is being forced to choose between two highly flawed narratives. Was OpenAI hijacked by Sam Altman to build a Microsoft monopoly, or was it a lifeboat that barely escaped Elon Musk's attempt to absorb it into the Tesla and SpaceX empire? 

With OpenAI's historic IPO hanging in the balance, every word spoken in the Oakland courthouse is actively reshaping the financial future of the tech sector.

## FAQ

**Did OpenAI employees actually work for Tesla for free?**
According to Brockman's sworn testimony, yes. In 2017, several OpenAI engineers were allegedly diverted to work on overhauling Tesla's Autopilot vision systems. Musk's team has yet to successfully rebut this specific claim.

**How is OpenAI paying a $50 billion computing bill?**
The massive $50 billion figure for 2026 relies heavily on their strategic partnership with Microsoft, massive enterprise licensing deals for new models, and multi-billion-dollar credit facilities. It highlights exactly why OpenAI's upcoming IPO is an existential necessity for the company.

**Why did Greg Brockman's diary become a huge issue?**
During discovery, Musk's lawyers found Brockman's 2017 diary, where he wrote it would be "wrong to steal the non-profit from [Musk]." Musk is using this to prove premeditated deceit, while Brockman claims the quotes are cherry-picked and taken wildly out of context. 

**What happens if the judge decides Musk is right?**
If the court rules that the pivot to a capped-profit model was an illegal breach of the founding agreement, the judge could force OpenAI to revert to its non-profit roots, open-source its proprietary models, or pay massive financial damages, potentially derailing their public offering entirely.

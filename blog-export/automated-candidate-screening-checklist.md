## 1. Why You Need an Automated Screening Checklist

Candidate screening without a checklist is like cooking without a recipe. You might get it right — but you'll get it right inconsistently, and you'll make avoidable mistakes.

An automated screening checklist does three things:

**1. Ensures consistency.** Every candidate is screened against the same criteria, in the same order, using the same tools. This prevents the arbitrary variation that leads to inconsistent hiring decisions.

**2. Reduces bias.** A structured checklist forces evaluators to apply the same criteria to every candidate — making it harder for unconscious bias to influence the screening decision.

**3. Saves time.** A well-designed automated screening process reduces the time spent on manual screening by 70–80%. The checklist ensures that every step of the automation is set up correctly and working as intended.

This guide provides a complete automated candidate screening checklist — covering every stage from job posting to shortlist. Use it as a template for every hire.

## 2. Pre-Screening Setup Checklist

Complete this checklist before you post the job. Getting the setup right is the most important step — everything downstream depends on it.

### Job Definition

- [ ] **Role outcomes defined.** Written down the specific outcomes expected in the first 30, 60, and 90 days.
- [ ] **Required skills identified.** Listed the 5–7 specific, job-relevant skills required for the role.
- [ ] **Nice-to-have skills identified.** Listed the skills that are desirable but not required.
- [ ] **Knockout criteria defined.** Identified the 3–5 must-have requirements that are genuinely disqualifying (work authorization, specific certification, minimum proficiency in a required technology).
- [ ] **Salary range confirmed.** Confirmed the salary range with the hiring manager and finance team.
- [ ] **Equity / benefits confirmed.** Confirmed the equity and benefits package.

---

### Job Description

- [ ] **Skills-based language used.** Job description lists specific skills and outcomes, not credentials and years of experience.
- [ ] **Tech stack specified** (for technical roles). Job description lists the exact languages, frameworks, and tools required.
- [ ] **Salary range included.** Salary range is included in the job description.
- [ ] **Hiring process described.** Job description explains the hiring process (assessment, async video, interview) so candidates know what to expect.
- [ ] **Bias-free language checked.** Job description has been reviewed for gendered or exclusionary language (use Textio or Gender Decoder).
- [ ] **Skills-first statement included.** Job description includes a statement that signals your commitment to skills-first hiring: "We evaluate candidates on demonstrated skills, not credentials."

---

### ATS Configuration

- [ ] **ATS account active.** ATS (Workable, Breezy HR, Ashby, or similar) is set up and configured.
- [ ] **Job posting created in ATS.** Job posting is live in the ATS and linked to the job board postings.
- [ ] **Knockout questions configured.** 3–5 knockout questions are added to the application form with automatic pass/fail logic.
- [ ] **Rejection email configured.** Automated rejection email is set up for candidates who fail knockout questions. Email is polite and professional.
- [ ] **Assessment invitation configured.** Automated assessment invitation is set up for candidates who pass knockout questions. Email explains what the assessment covers, how long it takes, and why you use it.
- [ ] **Stage-transition automations configured.** Automated emails are set up for every stage transition (application received, assessment completed, video completed, interview scheduled, rejection).
- [ ] **ATS-assessment integration tested.** Integration between ATS and assessment tool is tested and working (scores flow automatically from assessment tool to ATS).

---

### Skills Assessment Configuration

- [ ] **Assessment tool selected.** Assessment tool (TestGorilla, Vervoe, Codility, HackerRank, or similar) is selected based on the role type.
- [ ] **Assessment built or selected.** Assessment is built (custom) or selected (pre-built) and configured for the role.
- [ ] **Assessment length confirmed.** Assessment is 30–45 minutes maximum. Longer assessments have significantly lower completion rates.
- [ ] **Score threshold set.** Minimum score threshold is set (typically 60th–70th percentile). Candidates above threshold are automatically advanced. Candidates below threshold receive an automated rejection email.
- [ ] **Accommodation statement included.** Assessment invitation includes a statement offering accommodations to candidates with disabilities.
- [ ] **Assessment tested.** Assessment has been completed by a team member to verify that it works correctly and takes the expected amount of time.
- [ ] **Blind review enabled.** Identifying information (name, school, company) is removed from assessment results before review (if supported by the tool).

---

### Async Video Configuration

- [ ] **Async video tool selected.** Async video tool (Willo, Spark Hire, or similar) is selected.
- [ ] **Questions written.** 3 structured questions are written for the async video:
  - Question 1: Experience question ("Tell me about the experience most relevant to this role...")
  - Question 2: Motivation question ("Why are you interested in this role at [Company]?")
  - Question 3: Role-specific question (scenario or judgment question relevant to the role)
- [ ] **Time limits set.** Each question has a time limit (target: 2–3 minutes per question).
- [ ] **Scoring rubric built.** Scoring rubric is built for the async video (1–5 on each question).
- [ ] **Invitation configured.** Automated async video invitation is configured (triggered by assessment score above threshold).
- [ ] **Async video tested.** Async video has been completed by a team member to verify that it works correctly.

---

### Interview Configuration

- [ ] **Interview format defined.** Interview format is defined (number of rounds, duration, format — live video, phone, or in-person).
- [ ] **Interview questions written.** Structured behavioral interview questions are written for each competency.
- [ ] **Scoring rubric built.** Scoring rubric is built for each interview question (1–5 with behavioral anchors).
- [ ] **Scorecard template created.** Scorecard template is created and shared with all interviewers.
- [ ] **Scheduling tool configured.** Scheduling tool (Calendly or similar) is configured with available interview slots.
- [ ] **Interviewers briefed.** All interviewers have been briefed on the role, the evaluation criteria, and the scoring rubric.

## 3. Application Stage Checklist

Complete this checklist when the job is live and applications are coming in.

### Application Monitoring

- [ ] **Knockout questions are working.** Verify that candidates who fail knockout questions are being automatically rejected and receiving the rejection email.
- [ ] **Assessment invitations are being sent.** Verify that candidates who pass knockout questions are receiving the assessment invitation within 24 hours.
- [ ] **Application volume is tracked.** Track the number of applications received per day / per week.
- [ ] **Source of applications is tracked.** Track which job boards and channels are producing the most applications (and the highest-quality applications).

### Application Quality Review (Weekly)

- [ ] **Knockout question pass rate reviewed.** What percentage of applicants are passing all knockout questions? If the pass rate is below 30%, the knockout questions may be too restrictive. If the pass rate is above 80%, the knockout questions may not be filtering effectively.
- [ ] **Application quality spot-checked.** Review a sample of 10–20 applications to verify that the knockout questions are filtering correctly and that the right candidates are advancing.
- [ ] **Job description adjusted if needed.** If the application quality is poor (too many unqualified candidates), review and tighten the job description and knockout questions.

---

## 4. Skills Assessment Stage Checklist

Complete this checklist during the skills assessment stage.

### Assessment Monitoring

- [ ] **Assessment completion rate tracked.** What percentage of candidates who receive the assessment invitation are completing it? Target: 70%+. If completion rate is below 50%, the assessment may be too long, too difficult, or poorly communicated.
- [ ] **Assessment scores reviewed.** Review the distribution of assessment scores. If most candidates are scoring very high or very low, the assessment may be miscalibrated.
- [ ] **Threshold candidates flagged.** Candidates who score within 5–10 percentile points of the threshold are flagged for manual review (they may be worth advancing despite scoring below threshold).
- [ ] **Automated rejections verified.** Verify that candidates who score below threshold are receiving the automated rejection email.
- [ ] **Automated video invitations verified.** Verify that candidates who score above threshold are receiving the async video invitation.

### Assessment Quality Review (Per Hire)

- [ ] **Adverse impact checked.** Review whether any demographic group is passing the assessment at a significantly lower rate than others. If adverse impact is detected, investigate and address it.
- [ ] **Assessment relevance confirmed.** Verify that the assessment is measuring the skills that are actually required for the role. If top-scoring candidates are consistently failing in the role, the assessment may be measuring the wrong skills.

## 5. Async Video Stage Checklist

Complete this checklist during the async video stage.

### Video Review Setup

- [ ] **Videos reviewed in batches.** Block 45-minute sessions to review videos in batches of 10. Don't review videos one at a time as they come in.
- [ ] **Scoring rubric open during review.** Have the scoring rubric open during video review to ensure consistent scoring.
- [ ] **Score each video before watching the next.** Score each video immediately after watching it — before watching the next video. This prevents recency bias.
- [ ] **Identifying information ignored.** Focus on the content of the response, not the candidate's appearance, accent, or background.

### Video Review Checklist (Per Candidate)

- [ ] **Question 1 scored.** Experience question scored on 1–5 rubric. Evidence noted.
- [ ] **Question 2 scored.** Motivation question scored on 1–5 rubric. Evidence noted.
- [ ] **Question 3 scored.** Role-specific question scored on 1–5 rubric. Evidence noted.
- [ ] **Composite video score calculated.** Total score calculated (sum of question scores, or weighted average if questions have different weights).
- [ ] **Advancement decision made.** Candidate advanced to live interview (if above threshold) or rejected (if below threshold).

### Video Stage Monitoring

- [ ] **Video completion rate tracked.** What percentage of candidates who receive the video invitation are completing it? Target: 70%+.
- [ ] **Video score distribution reviewed.** Review the distribution of video scores. If most candidates are scoring very high or very low, the questions or rubric may need adjustment.

## 6. Interview Stage Checklist

Complete this checklist during the interview stage.

### Interview Scheduling

- [ ] **Interview invitations sent within 24 hours.** Candidates who pass the video stage receive an interview invitation within 24 hours.
- [ ] **Self-scheduling link included.** Interview invitation includes a self-scheduling link (Calendly or similar) so candidates can book a time without back-and-forth email.
- [ ] **Interview confirmation sent.** Automated confirmation email is sent when the candidate books a time.
- [ ] **Interview reminder sent.** Automated reminder email is sent 24 hours before the interview.

### Pre-Interview Preparation (Per Candidate)

- [ ] **Candidate's assessment score reviewed.** Review the candidate's assessment score and any flagged areas before the interview.
- [ ] **Candidate's async video reviewed.** Review the candidate's async video responses before the interview.
- [ ] **Interview questions prepared.** Have the structured interview questions and scoring rubric ready before the interview.
- [ ] **Candidate's application reviewed.** Review the candidate's application (not the resume — the application form responses) before the interview.

### During the Interview

- [ ] **Interview started on time.** Interview started within 5 minutes of the scheduled time.
- [ ] **Structured questions asked.** All structured interview questions were asked (same questions for every candidate).
- [ ] **Follow-up questions asked.** Follow-up questions were asked to probe for specific evidence ("Can you give me a specific example?" "What was the outcome?").
- [ ] **Notes taken.** Notes were taken during the interview (specific quotes and examples, not general impressions).
- [ ] **Candidate given time to ask questions.** Candidate was given 5–10 minutes to ask questions at the end of the interview.

### Post-Interview (Within 24 Hours)

- [ ] **Scorecard completed.** Scorecard completed within 24 hours of the interview, while the interview is fresh.
- [ ] **Evidence cited for each score.** Each score on the scorecard is supported by specific evidence from the interview (not general impressions).
- [ ] **Overall recommendation made.** Overall recommendation made (Strong Hire / Hire / No Hire / Strong No Hire).
- [ ] **Scorecard submitted.** Scorecard submitted to the hiring manager or ATS.

## 7. Decision Stage Checklist

Complete this checklist when making the final hiring decision.

### Debrief Meeting

- [ ] **All interviewers present.** All interviewers attend the debrief meeting (or submit written feedback if they can't attend).
- [ ] **Scorecards reviewed before discussion.** All interviewers review each other's scorecards before the discussion begins.
- [ ] **Evidence-based discussion.** Discussion focuses on specific evidence from the candidate's work — not general impressions.
- [ ] **Disagreements resolved.** Significant disagreements between interviewers are discussed and resolved.
- [ ] **Decision made.** A clear hiring decision is made (hire or no hire) for each candidate.
- [ ] **Top candidate identified.** If multiple candidates are being considered, the top candidate is identified based on composite scores.

### Offer Stage

- [ ] **Offer extended within 48 hours.** Offer is extended within 48 hours of the hiring decision.
- [ ] **Offer details confirmed.** Salary, equity, start date, and benefits are confirmed before the offer is extended.
- [ ] **Offer letter sent.** Formal offer letter is sent via email.
- [ ] **Rejection emails sent.** Polite rejection emails are sent to all candidates who were not selected, within 24 hours of the hiring decision.

## 8. Post-Hire Checklist

Complete this checklist after the hire is made, to improve your screening process for future hires.

### Process Review (Within 30 Days of Hire)

- [ ] **Time to hire calculated.** Calculate the total time from job posting to offer acceptance. Compare to your target (21 days or fewer).
- [ ] **Screening time calculated.** Calculate the total time spent on manual screening per hire. Compare to your target (4 hours or fewer).
- [ ] **Completion rates reviewed.** Review the assessment and async video completion rates. Identify any stages where candidates are dropping out.
- [ ] **Adverse impact reviewed.** Review the pass rates at each stage by demographic group. Identify any stages where adverse impact may be occurring.
- [ ] **Process improvements identified.** Identify 1–3 specific improvements to the screening process for the next hire.

### Quality of Hire Review (At 30, 60, and 90 Days)

- [ ] **30-day check-in completed.** Hiring manager completes a structured 30-day performance check-in with the new hire.
- [ ] **60-day check-in completed.** Hiring manager completes a structured 60-day performance check-in.
- [ ] **90-day performance review completed.** Hiring manager completes a structured 90-day performance review. New hire is rated on the outcomes defined in the Role Definition Document.
- [ ] **Assessment predictive validity reviewed.** Compare the new hire's assessment score to their 90-day performance rating. Over time, this data will tell you whether your assessment is predicting performance accurately.

## 9. Automated Screening Tools Checklist

Use this checklist to select and configure the right tools for your automated screening process.

### ATS Selection

- [ ] **ATS supports knockout questions** with automatic pass/fail logic
- [ ] **ATS integrates with your assessment tool** (native integration or Zapier)
- [ ] **ATS integrates with your async video tool**
- [ ] **ATS supports automated stage-transition emails**
- [ ] **ATS provides reporting** on time to hire, stage conversion rates, and source of hire
- [ ] **ATS supports blind candidate review** (anonymization of identifying information)

**Recommended tools:** Workable, Breezy HR, Ashby, Greenhouse

---

### Assessment Tool Selection

- [ ] **Assessment covers the specific skills required for the role**
- [ ] **Assessment has published validation data** (predictive validity and adverse impact)
- [ ] **Assessment is fully async** (candidates can complete on their own schedule)
- [ ] **Assessment integrates with your ATS**
- [ ] **Assessment supports blind review** (remove identifying information from results)
- [ ] **Assessment provides percentile scores** (so you can set a threshold without understanding the raw scores)
- [ ] **Assessment length is 30–45 minutes maximum**

**Recommended tools:** TestGorilla (general), Vervoe (work samples), Codility/HackerRank (engineering)

---

### Async Video Tool Selection

- [ ] **Tool is fully async** (candidates record on their own schedule)
- [ ] **Tool supports structured questions** (define exactly what you want to ask)
- [ ] **Tool supports team review** (multiple reviewers can watch and score the same video)
- [ ] **Tool integrates with your ATS**
- [ ] **Tool provides a good candidate experience** (clean interface, mobile-friendly)
- [ ] **Tool supports automated invitations** (triggered by assessment score threshold)

**Recommended tools:** Willo, Spark Hire

---

### Scheduling Tool Selection

- [ ] **Tool supports self-scheduling** (candidates can book a time without back-and-forth email)
- [ ] **Tool integrates with your calendar** (Google Calendar, Outlook)
- [ ] **Tool sends automated reminders** to candidates and interviewers
- [ ] **Tool supports multiple interview formats** (video, phone, in-person)

**Recommended tools:** Calendly, Acuity Scheduling

## 10. Common Automated Screening Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

### Mistake 1: Knockout Questions That Are Too Restrictive

If your knockout questions eliminate 80%+ of applicants, they may be too restrictive — filtering out qualified candidates based on requirements that aren't genuinely disqualifying.

**Fix:** Review your knockout questions. For each question, ask: "Is this truly a disqualifying factor, or is it just a preference?" Remove any questions that are preferences rather than requirements.

---

### Mistake 2: Assessments That Are Too Long

Assessments longer than 45 minutes have significantly lower completion rates — especially for passive candidates (candidates who are currently employed and have limited time). A 90-minute assessment will be completed by only the most motivated candidates — which may or may not be the best candidates.

**Fix:** Keep assessments to 30–45 minutes maximum. If you need to evaluate more skills, use multiple shorter assessments at different stages of the process.

---

### Mistake 3: No Accommodation Process

Candidates with disabilities may need accommodations to complete your assessment — extended time, alternative format, or assistive technology. If you don't offer accommodations, you may be excluding qualified candidates and exposing yourself to legal liability.

**Fix:** Include a statement in your assessment invitation offering accommodations. Set up a process for handling accommodation requests quickly (within 24 hours).

---

### Mistake 4: Automated Rejections Without Human Review

Fully automated rejection (rejecting candidates based solely on assessment scores without any human review) can produce adverse impact and miss qualified candidates who scored just below the threshold.

**Fix:** Review candidates who score within 5–10 percentile points of the threshold manually. Don't reject candidates automatically without any human review.

---

### Mistake 5: Not Monitoring Completion Rates

If candidates are dropping out of your screening process at a high rate, something is wrong — the assessment is too long, the instructions are unclear, the candidate experience is poor, or the communication is confusing. But if you're not monitoring completion rates, you won't know.

**Fix:** Track completion rates at every stage. If completion rates drop below 70%, investigate why and fix it.

---

### Mistake 6: Not Measuring Quality of Hire

The ultimate measure of your screening process is whether it produces great hires. If you're not measuring quality of hire (90-day performance ratings), you have no way to know whether your screening process is working.

**Fix:** Implement a structured 90-day performance review process. Compare performance ratings to screening scores. Use this data to continuously improve your screening process.

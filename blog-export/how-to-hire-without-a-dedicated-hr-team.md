## 1. The HR-Free Hiring Reality

For most startups, "HR" is not a person or a department. It's the founder, late at night, trying to figure out how to post a job on LinkedIn, screen 200 resumes, schedule interviews, and draft an offer letter — all while running the company.

This is distinct from "recruiting." Recruiting is finding the person. "HR" is the machinery: the compliance, the documentation, the offers, the onboarding. When you don't have an HR team, the risk of compliance errors, administrative burnout, and poor candidate experience is high.

But you don't need a dedicated HR person to have a professional hiring process. You need a system. A system that automates the administrative work, ensures compliance by default, and creates a seamless experience for candidates — without requiring 20 hours of founder time per week.

This guide is the playbook for hiring without an HR team — covering the tools, templates, and processes that replace the need for a dedicated HR hire until you reach 50+ employees.

## 2. The "HR in a Box" Tech Stack

You can't replace a person with nothing. You replace them with software. Here is the minimum viable "HR in a Box" stack for early-stage startups.

### 1. The System of Record: ATS (Applicant Tracking System)
**Don't use:** Email folders and spreadsheets.
**Do use:** A free or low-cost ATS (Breezy HR, Workable, Ashby).
**Why:** It handles compliance (GDPR/CCPA), automated communication, and document storage. It is your legal defense if a hiring decision is ever challenged.

### 2. The Compliance Engine: Offer & Onboarding Platform
**Don't use:** Word documents and manual PDFs.
**Do use:** Gusto, Rippling, or Deel (for remote/international).
**Why:** These platforms generate compliant offer letters, handle state tax registrations (a nightmare for remote teams), run background checks, and onboard employees into payroll automatically. They ensure you don't accidentally break employment laws.

### 3. The Scheduling Coordinator: Automation Tool
**Don't use:** " What time works for you?" emails.
**Do use:** Calendly or SavvyCal.
**Why:** It eliminates the manual back-and-forth that eats up founder time.

### 4. The Screening Assistant: Skills Assessment
**Don't use:** Resume screening (requires human time).
**Do use:** TestGorilla or Vervoe.
**Why:** It screens candidates automatically based on skills, ranking them for you so you only talk to the qualified ones.

## 3. The Compliant Hiring Process (Step-by-Step)

Without HR, compliance is your responsibility. Follow this process to stay safe.

### Step 1: Defined Role & Salary (The "Equal Pay" Step)
Many states (CA, NY, WA, CO) now require salary ranges in job postings. Even if you're not in those states, posting a remote role opens you to their laws.
*   **Action:** Define the salary band *before* you post. Document the skills required.
*   **Compliance Check:** Does the job description use gender-neutral language? (Use a free tool like Gender Decoder).

### Step 2: Structured Interviewing (The "Anti-Bias" Step)
Unstructured interviews ("so, tell me about yourself") are legal minefields. You might accidentally ask about protected characteristics (family, age, origin).
*   **Action:** Create a standard interview guide with specific questions for every candidate.
*   **Compliance Check:** Do not ask about: age, marital status, kids, religion, or current salary history (illegal in many states).

### Step 3: Documented Decision Making (The "Defense" Step)
If a candidate claims discrimination, your defense is your documentation.
*   **Action:** Fill out a scorecard for every interview immediately. Focus on evidence ("candidate demonstrated X skill"), not opinion ("I liked them").
*   **Compliance Check:** Keep all interview notes in your ATS, not on loose paper or personal notes apps.

### Step 4: Background Checks (The "Safety" Step)
Don't Google candidates. It's unreliable and can expose you to information you can't legally discover (like expunged records).
*   **Action:** Use a compliant background check service (Checkr, integrated with your ATS or payroll).
*   **Compliance Check:** You must get the candidate's consent before running a background check (FCRA requirement).

## 4. Drafting the Offer Letter (Without a Lawyer)

The offer letter is a legal contract. Don't write it from scratch.

### Essential Elements:
1.  **At-Will Statement:** Crucial in the US. deeply implies either party can end employment at any time.
2.  **Compensation:** State salary as a "per pay period" amount, annualized for reference. (Avoids "guaranteed annual salary" contract disputes).
3.  **Equity:** Reference the specific Stock Option Plan and number of shares (or value), noting it is subject to Board approval.
4.  **Benefits:** High-level summary (e.g., "eligible for standard company benefits").
5.  **Contingencies:** "Offer is contingent upon successful completion of background check and reference checks."

**Pro Tip:** Use the templates inside Gusto/Rippling. They are updated by lawyers for state-specific requirements.

## 5. Onboarding: The First Week

HR usually handles the "first day" logistics. Without HR, you need a checklist.

**The "Day 0" Checklist (Before they start):**
*   [ ] Laptop ordered and shipped (use an IT management platform like Rippling if possible)
*   [ ] Accounts created (Email, Slack, GitHub, Notion)
*   [ ] First week meetings scheduled on their calendar
*   [ ] "Welcome Guide" emailed (links to handbook, mission, values)

**The "Day 1" Checklist:**
*   [ ] I-9 Verification (Federal requirement). Do this immediately. Remote employees can use specific authorized representatives or services.
*   [ ] Employee Handbook signed (acknowledgment of policies).
*   [ ] Direct deposit set up.

## 6. Founder-Led Culture

The biggest "HR" job isn't paperwork—it's culture. Without an HR leader, the founder IS the culture.

*   **1-on-1s:** You must schedule recurring 1-on-1s. This is your primary retention tool.
*   **Feedback Loops:** Create a mechanism for feedback (e.g., monthly simplified engagement survey using Google Forms or Culture Amp).
*   **Performance Reviews:** Keep it simple. "Keep, Stop, Start" every quarter is better than a complex annual review.

## 7. When to Finally Hire HR

You can manage this "HR-free" setup until about 30–50 employees.
**Signs you need a dedicated HR hire:**
*   You are spending >20% of your time on "people issues" (conflict resolution, policy questions).
*   You represent compliance risks (hiring in multiple international jurisdictions).
*   Employee retention starts to dip.
*   You can't keep up with the recruiting volume.

Until then, automate the admin, template the process, and lead the culture yourself.

## What Is an ATS and How Does It Actually Work?

Before we fix your resume, you need to understand what you are up against.

An Applicant Tracking System is software that companies use to collect, sort, scan, and rank incoming job applications. When you hit "apply" on LinkedIn, Naukri, or a company's careers page — your resume enters an ATS, not a recruiter's inbox.

Here is what the ATS does in those first few seconds:

**Step 1 — Parse your resume:** The ATS reads your resume and extracts information into structured fields. Name, email, phone, work experience, education, skills. If your formatting confuses the parser — complex tables, multiple columns, graphics, unusual fonts — the information gets extracted incorrectly or missed entirely.

**Step 2 — Keyword match:** The ATS compares your resume against the job description, looking for keywords — specific skills, tools, certifications, and job titles. The more your resume matches the keywords in the job description, the higher your score.

**Step 3 — Rank and filter:** Candidates are ranked by score. Only the top-scoring resumes get forwarded to the recruiter. Everyone below the threshold is automatically rejected, often without any notification.

The key insight: **a visually beautiful resume with poor ATS compatibility will be rejected before a human sees it. A plain, keyword-optimized resume will be shortlisted.** In 2026, the ATS does not care about your Canva design.

## The 8 Non-Negotiable ATS Formatting Rules

These are the rules that determine whether your resume gets parsed correctly. Break any of them and you risk automatic rejection regardless of your qualifications.

### Rule 1: Use a Single-Column Layout

ATS systems read resumes from top to bottom, left to right, in a single pass. Multi-column layouts — where information is split into side-by-side columns — confuse the reading order. The ATS may read across both columns simultaneously, mixing up your work experience dates with your skills or contact information.

Single column. Always. No exceptions for ATS applications.

### Rule 2: Use Standard Fonts Only

Stick to: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Times New Roman, or Georgia. Font size 10–12pt for body text, 14–16pt for your name.

Fancy or decorative fonts — including fonts embedded in Canva templates — often fail to parse correctly. The ATS sees garbled characters or blank spaces where your text should be.

### Rule 3: Use Standard Section Headings

ATS systems find sections of your resume by looking for standard headings. Use exactly these headings:
- **Work Experience** (not "My Journey" or "Where I've Been")
- **Education** (not "Academic Background")
- **Skills** (not "What I Bring to the Table")
- **Certifications** (not "Achievements")
- **Projects** (standard)

Custom creative headings sound clever to humans. ATS systems cannot find the section and skip it entirely.

### Rule 4: No Tables, Text Boxes, or Graphics

Tables and text boxes are invisible to most ATS systems. If you put your contact information in a header text box — which many Word templates do — the ATS may not extract your email or phone number at all. If you list your skills in a table, the ATS may not read them.

Keep everything in plain text, in the main body of the document.

### Rule 5: No Photos, Icons, or Visual Elements

Photos waste space and break ATS parsing. The ATS cannot read a photo and the image data confuses the parser. Leave photos off entirely — for IT and private company applications in India, a photo adds no value and creates ATS problems.

The same applies to progress bars showing "Python — 80%" or icons beside your skills. The ATS reads the icon code, not the skill name.

### Rule 6: Save in the Right File Format

Most ATS systems prefer .docx (Microsoft Word format). Many ATS systems cannot read PDFs accurately, especially PDFs created from Canva or design tools.

Unless the job posting specifically says "send PDF," submit as .docx. If both are acceptable, submit .docx.

### Rule 7: Put Contact Information in Plain Text at the Top

Your name, phone number, email, LinkedIn URL, and city should be in plain text at the very top of your resume — not in a header, footer, or text box. Many ATS platforms skip headers and footers entirely when parsing.

Use a professional email: firstname.lastname@gmail.com. Not coolboy99@gmail.com.

### Rule 8: Keep Consistent Formatting Throughout

Inconsistent date formats (sometimes "Jan 2024", sometimes "01/2024"), inconsistent bullet styles, and inconsistent spacing all increase the chance of parsing errors. Pick one format for everything and use it throughout.

## The Keyword Strategy That Actually Gets You Shortlisted

Formatting gets your resume parsed correctly. Keywords get you shortlisted.

Here is the process that works:

### Step 1: Mine the Job Description

Read the job description carefully and extract every specific skill, tool, technology, and qualification mentioned. These are your target keywords.

Example — for a Digital Marketing Executive role:
- SEO, SEM, Google Ads, Meta Ads
- Google Analytics, Google Search Console
- Email marketing, Mailchimp
- Content marketing, social media marketing
- A/B testing, conversion rate optimization

### Step 2: Identify Your Keyword Gaps

Compare the job description keywords against what is currently in your resume. Every keyword in the job description that is absent from your resume is a missed match point.

### Step 3: Add Keywords Naturally — Not as a List

The ATS in 2026 has evolved. It does not just look for isolated keyword matches. It looks for keywords in context — in sentences that show how you used the skill.

❌ **Wrong:** Skills: SEO, SEM, Google Ads, Content Marketing

✅ **Right:** "Increased organic traffic by 45% through SEO optimization, keyword clustering, and technical audits using Google Search Console and SEMrush."

The second version contains multiple keywords in a way that makes sense to both the ATS and the human recruiter who reads it afterward.

### Step 4: Include Both Full Forms and Abbreviations

ATS systems are not always smart about matching abbreviations to full forms. Include both versions where relevant.

Example: "Artificial Intelligence (AI)" — "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)" — "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"

### Step 5: Naukri-Specific Tip

On Naukri.com, pay special attention to the "Key Skills" tags at the bottom of job postings. These are the exact terms recruiters enter when searching the Naukri database. Match these tags in both your resume and your Naukri profile to dramatically improve your visibility in recruiter searches.

## Section-by-Section Resume Guide for 2026

### Contact Information (Top of Page)

Include: Full name, phone number, professional email, LinkedIn URL, city and state (not full address).

Do not include: Date of birth, father's name, marital status, religion, photo. These are outdated requirements in private sector hiring and can introduce bias.

### Professional Summary (3–4 Lines)

This is the first thing both the ATS and the recruiter read. Front-load it with keywords.

❌ **Wrong:** "Hardworking and dedicated professional looking for a challenging opportunity to grow."

✅ **Right:** "Digital Marketing Executive with 2 years of experience in SEO, Google Ads, and Meta advertising. Increased organic traffic by 60% for a B2B SaaS company. Skilled in Google Analytics 4, content marketing, and email automation using Mailchimp."

Every sentence should contain a keyword from your target job description.

### Work Experience (Reverse Chronological)

List most recent job first. For each role include: Job title, company name, location, start and end date (Month Year format), and 3–5 bullet points of achievements.

The most important rule for bullet points: **quantify everything possible.**

❌ "Managed social media accounts"
✅ "Managed Instagram and LinkedIn accounts for a D2C brand, growing combined followers from 8,000 to 45,000 in 9 months with 12% average engagement rate"

Numbers make your achievements real for both ATS and humans.

### Skills Section

This is your keyword bank. Be comprehensive — list every relevant technology, tool, and skill you have genuine knowledge of. Group them logically:

- **Technical Skills:** Python, JavaScript, React.js, SQL, AWS
- **Marketing Tools:** Google Ads, Meta Business Suite, HubSpot, Mailchimp
- **Soft Skills:** Project management, cross-functional collaboration, data-driven decision making

Only list skills you can actually discuss in an interview. A skill on your resume that you cannot speak to in an interview creates a negative impression that is worse than not listing it.

### Education

Degree name, university, year of graduation, and percentage/CGPA. For freshers, education comes near the top — it is your strongest credential. For experienced professionals, education moves to the bottom after work experience.

### Projects (Critical for Freshers)

If you have limited work experience, projects are where you embed your keywords. Describe each project with the technologies used, your specific role, and measurable outcomes.

❌ "Made a website for college project"
✅ "Developed a responsive e-commerce web application using React.js, Node.js, and MongoDB with payment gateway integration (Razorpay) — processed 200+ test transactions during UAT"

### Certifications

List certification name, issuing organization, and year. Free certifications from Google (Google Analytics, Digital Marketing), Coursera, AWS, Microsoft, and HubSpot are widely recognized and ATS-searchable.

## Fresher-Specific ATS Resume Guide

Freshers face a unique challenge: you need to pass keyword screening without work experience to embed keywords in.

The solution is strategic placement:

**Resume Objective** (not summary) — 3 lines with your target role, key skills, and value proposition:
"B.Tech CSE graduate with strong Python and SQL skills and 2 completed web development projects seeking a Software Developer role where I can contribute to building scalable applications."

**Section Order for Freshers:**
1. Contact Information
2. Career Objective
3. Education
4. Technical Skills (keyword-heavy)
5. Projects (proof of applied skills)
6. Internships (if any)
7. Certifications
8. Extra-curriculars

**The keyword workaround for freshers:** You cannot embed keywords in work experience bullets. So maximize them everywhere else. Your Skills section should be comprehensive. Your Project descriptions should use the exact terminology from job descriptions. Your Certifications section should list every relevant course you have completed.

**Length:** One page maximum for freshers. Every line must earn its space.

## The 10 Most Common ATS Mistakes (And How to Fix Them)

**Mistake 1: Using a Canva template**
Fix: Use a plain Word document or Google Doc with a simple, single-column layout.

**Mistake 2: Contact info in header/footer**
Fix: Move all contact information to the main body at the top of the page.

**Mistake 3: Creative section headings**
Fix: Use standard headings — Work Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications.

**Mistake 4: Listing skills without context**
Fix: Embed skills in achievement bullet points with quantified results.

**Mistake 5: Not customizing for each job**
Fix: Customize your Professional Summary and Skills section for each application by matching keywords from that specific job description.

**Mistake 6: Using tables for skills or contact info**
Fix: Delete all tables and reformat as plain bulleted or comma-separated text.

**Mistake 7: Inconsistent date formats**
Fix: Choose one format — "March 2024" or "Mar 2024" — and use it throughout.

**Mistake 8: Submitting PDF from Canva**
Fix: Export as .docx whenever possible. If PDF is required, create it from Microsoft Word or Google Docs, not Canva.

**Mistake 9: No LinkedIn URL**
Fix: Add your LinkedIn profile URL. Many ATS platforms cross-reference LinkedIn data.

**Mistake 10: Lying or exaggerating skills**
Fix: Only list skills you can genuinely discuss in an interview. ATS gets you shortlisted — the interview is where you must back it up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is an ATS-friendly resume?**
An ATS-friendly resume uses a single-column layout, standard fonts, plain text formatting, and keyword-rich content matched to the job description — so Applicant Tracking Systems can accurately parse and rank it before a human recruiter reviews it.

**Q: Do Indian companies use ATS in 2026?**
Yes. 97% of Fortune 500 companies and 75% of mid-size employers globally use ATS. In India, Naukri.com (which processes 15 million+ active resumes), LinkedIn, and most large corporations including TCS, Infosys, Wipro, and Amazon India use ATS to filter applications.

**Q: Should I use a Canva resume template in 2026?**
No — for ATS-screened applications. Canva templates use multi-column layouts, graphics, icons, and invisible layers that most ATS systems cannot parse correctly. Use a plain single-column Word document for job applications. Save Canva resumes for networking events or LinkedIn where a human sees it directly.

**Q: How do I add keywords to my resume without keyword stuffing?**
Extract keywords from the specific job description and embed them naturally in your Professional Summary, Work Experience bullet points, and Skills section as part of sentences describing real achievements. Never list keywords in a block of unrelated text — ATS systems in 2026 evaluate context, not just keyword presence.

**Q: What is the best resume format for freshers in India 2026?**
For freshers: Career Objective → Education → Technical Skills → Projects → Internships → Certifications → Extra-curriculars. Single column, Arial or Calibri font 10–11pt, no photos or tables, saved as .docx. Keep it to one page with every line earning its space.

**Q: How long should my resume be in 2026?**
Freshers: 1 page maximum. 1–5 years experience: 1–2 pages. 5+ years experience: 2 pages maximum. Recruiters spend an average of 6–7 seconds on initial resume screening — concise, relevant content outperforms length every time.

**Q: Should I include a photo in my Indian resume?**
No — for IT and private sector jobs. Photos waste space, can introduce bias, and often break ATS parsing. The only exception is government job application forms that specifically request a photo.

**Q: How do I make my Naukri profile ATS-friendly?**
On Naukri, match your Key Skills tags to the exact terms listed in job postings you are targeting. Keep your headline keyword-rich with your target role. Keep your profile summary focused and role-specific. A complete, keyword-matched Naukri profile significantly improves your visibility in recruiter searches on the platform.

**Q: What file format should I submit my resume in?**
.docx (Microsoft Word) for maximum ATS compatibility. Submit as PDF only if the job posting specifically requires it or if you are applying to a company known to use modern ATS that handles PDFs well (such as Workday or Greenhouse). When in doubt, use .docx.

**Q: How can I check if my resume is ATS-compatible?**
Use Jobscan.co to compare your resume against a specific job description and get a keyword match score. Alternatively, paste your resume into a plain text editor — if the content reads logically with nothing scrambled, the basic parse test passes. Hirenest also provides a resume score alongside skill assessments that shows how your profile compares to role requirements.

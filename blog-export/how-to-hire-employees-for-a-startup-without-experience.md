## 1. The "Imposter Syndrome" of First-Time Hiring

You've built a product, raised some money (or bootstrapped), and now you need a team.
But looking at a resume feels like reading a foreign language. You think: *"Who am I to judge this senior engineer? I've never managed anyone."*

This is normal. **Most founders are terrible at hiring initially.**
They hire friends. They hire based on "vibes." They hire the first person who says yes.

**The Good News:** Hiring is a *skill*, not a talent. You can learn it fast.
This guide chops the massive, scary "Hiring" monster into small, manageable steps for the first-time founder.

---

## 2. Step 1: Admit What You Don't Know (The Scorecard)

Don't write a job description yet. You'll just copy-paste one from Google, and it will be wrong.
Instead, write a **Scorecard**.

**The "Job Outcome" Exercise:**
Imagine it's one year from now. You are giving this person a high-five because they crushed it. *What exactly did they do?*
*   *Vague:* "They handled marketing."
*   *Specific:* "They launched 3 campaigns, grew our email list to 10k, and automated our weekly newsletter."

**Action:** Write down 3-5 specific outcomes. These become your **Hiring Criteria**.
If a candidate can prove they've done these 3 things before, you hire them. Simple.

---

## 3. Step 2: Sourcing (The "Network First" Strategy)

Since you have no experience assessing strangers, **don't start with strangers.**
Hire from your network (or 2nd-degree network) first.

**Why?**
*   **Trust:** Your friend "John" won't vouch for a total disaster.
*   **Speed:** No need for months of screening.
*   **Verification:** You can get the "real" reference check over coffee.

**The "Ask":**
Don't post "We're hiring!" on LinkedIn. It's too generic.
Message 20 people: *"Who is the best frontend developer you know who might be bored right now?"*

---

## 4. Step 3: The "Work Sample" Shortcut

If you can't interview well, **don't interview.** (At least, not to judge competence).
Use a **Work Sample**.

**The Logic:**
You might face a smooth talker who knows nothing. You might face a nervous genius who stammers.
A work sample levels the playing field.

**Examples:**
*   *Marketing:* "Critique our landing page. What 3 things would you change?"
*   *Sales:* "Sell me this pen. Go."
*   *Dev:* "Here is a broken function. Fix it."

**Rule:** If they can do the work sample, they can do the job. If they can't, no amount of "good interview vibes" matters.

---

## 5. Step 4: The "Who" Interview (Topgrading)

You need to know if they are reliable. Use the **"Who" method** (from the book *Who*).

**The Only Question You Need:**
*"Walk me through your career history, starting from your first job. For each role, tell me: What were you hired to do? What did you achieve? Why did you leave?"*

**Look for:**
*   **Progression:** Are they getting promoted?
*   **Ownership:** Do they say "I" or "We"?
*   **Honesty:** Do their reasons for leaving make sense? (e.g., "My boss was a jerk" 3 times in a row is a red flag).

---

## 6. Step 5: Reference Checks (Your Safety Net)

Since you are inexperienced, you *must* rely on the experience of their past bosses.
**Never skip this.**

**The Script:**
*"I'm a first-time founder, so I'm really relying on your honesty here. If you were me, would you hire [Candidate] again? What specific advice would you give me to manage them best?"*

**Red Flag:** "They were... fine." (Silence/Hesitation is a "No").
**Green Flag:** "I'm trying to hire them back right now!"

---

## 7. Step 6: The "Trial Period" (De-Risking)

You will make mistakes. Plan for them.
Hire on a **Contract-to-Hire** basis (e.g., 30-day contract) or have a strict **90-day Probation Period.**
Be transparent: *"We are a startup. Speed and fit matter. Let's date before we marry."*

**Conclusion:**
You don't need 10 years of HR experience. You need:
1.  Clear Outcomes.
2.  A Work Sample.
3.  Honest References.
Stick to that loop, and you'll outperform 90% of "experienced" managers.

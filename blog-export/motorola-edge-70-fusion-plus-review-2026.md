**TL;DR (Direct Answer):** The Motorola Edge 70 Fusion+ is a mid-range Android phone that genuinely punches above its weight class. It carries a 6.8-inch 144Hz quad-curved AMOLED display, a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 processor, three 50MP cameras including a 3x optical telephoto, IP68 and IP69 water resistance, a 5,200mAh battery with 68W fast charging, and ships running Android 16 with a promise of three major OS upgrades and five years of security patches. It launched in Brazil at BRL 2,969 (roughly $570) with 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage. If you are spending significantly more than that on a smartphone right now, the Fusion+ is going to make you think hard about whether you actually need to.

---

## Why This Phone Deserves More Attention Than It's Getting

Motorola has been doing something quietly interesting for the past couple of years. While the rest of the industry has been laser-focused on racing to $1,200 flagship territory, Motorola has been methodically stacking real, meaningful features into phones that cost a fraction of that. The Edge 70 Fusion+ is the latest, and sharpest, expression of that strategy.

It arrived with almost no fanfare. Released in Brazil on March 12, 2026 — just days after the standard Edge 70 Fusion's global launch at MWC — the Fusion+ slots above its sibling with a notably upgraded processor and a completely different camera system. No splashy keynote, no months of teasers. Motorola essentially just listed it and moved on. That kind of quiet confidence is either a sign the company knows exactly who this phone is for, or a missed marketing opportunity. Probably both.

The timing is interesting too. Motorola launched the Edge 70 Fusion at MWC 2026 as a direct challenge to Samsung's Galaxy A56, Google's Pixel 10a, and the broader mid-range field. The Fusion+ is a follow-up shot, aimed slightly higher — and the spec sheet makes a compelling case that it lands.

---

## The Display: Hard to Fault at This Price

The screen is genuinely one of the Fusion+'s strongest selling points, and it sets a tone for the entire experience. You get a 6.8-inch Extreme AMOLED panel with a Super HD resolution, a 144Hz refresh rate, quad-curved edges, and a peak brightness of up to 5,200 nits. That last number is not a typo.

To put 5,200 nits in context: the iPhone 16 Pro peaks at around 2,000 nits. The Galaxy S26 Ultra, one of the brightest Android flagships available right now, peaks at around 2,600 nits for most real-world use. Motorola has shipped a mid-range phone with a screen that, on paper, gets significantly brighter than both of those. Real-world brightness depends on how the panel is driven and for how long, and peak nits under sustained load will vary — but the underlying hardware capability here is remarkable for the category.

The quad-curved design adds a premium tactile feel in the hand that typically costs you a lot more. The 144Hz refresh rate keeps everything smooth during scrolling and gaming. And the AMOLED panel means blacks are genuinely black, colours are vivid, and battery life doesn't suffer unnecessarily from always-on brightness.

---

## The Processor: Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 Does the Job

The Fusion+ runs on Qualcomm's Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 — the same chip found in the Poco M8 Pro 5G and Redmi Note 15 Pro Plus. It's not the Snapdragon 8 Elite powering this year's flagships, and it doesn't pretend to be. What it is: a genuinely capable mid-range chip that handles everyday workloads, sustained app usage, and moderate gaming without drama.

Paired with 12GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, the Fusion+ feels responsive. App launches are snappy, multitasking holds up, and you won't find yourself waiting on the phone in situations that matter. Where it will show its limits is in graphically demanding, sustained gaming sessions — this is not a chip designed for that use case. But for the overwhelming majority of what most people do with their phones — social media, streaming, photography, navigation, productivity — the 7s Gen 4 more than covers the ground.

The software experience deserves a mention here too. Motorola ships this phone with Android 16, which is the latest version available right now. Their Hello UI layer sits on top, and Motorola's philosophy here has always been close-to-stock Android with minimal interference. That means a clean, fast interface, very little bloatware, and an update experience that doesn't feel like it's fighting you. Three major OS upgrades and five years of security patches takes this phone through to 2031 — a long-term support commitment that many competitors in the category simply don't match.

---

## The Cameras: Three 50MP Sensors and a Real Telephoto

This is where the Fusion+ separates itself most clearly from the standard Edge 70 Fusion. While the base model ships with a 50MP main sensor and a 13MP ultrawide, the Fusion+ upgrades the entire camera array.

You get a 50MP primary camera using Sony's Lytia 710 sensor — the same capable sensor found in the base Fusion. Then a 50MP ultrawide. And then, in the move that makes the Fusion+ feel like a genuine step up, a 10MP 3x optical telephoto camera. That third lens is not something you typically see at this price point, and it changes the versatility of the phone meaningfully. Portraits at medium distance, compressed backgrounds, shooting subjects that are further away — these all benefit from having real optical zoom rather than relying on digital crop.

The front-facing camera also runs at 50MP. Motorola is clearly leading with camera specs as a selling point here, and for good reason. The triple-50MP configuration is an unusual marketing claim for the segment, and while raw megapixel counts don't tell the whole story of camera quality, they do indicate that Motorola has invested in hardware across all three lenses rather than putting one good sensor in the main slot and cutting corners elsewhere.

Whether real-world photo quality matches the spec sheet is something full long-term testing would settle definitively. What's already clear is that having a 3x optical telephoto on a sub-$600 phone in 2026 is genuinely notable.

## Build, Battery, and the Things That Actually Matter Day-to-Day

The Fusion+ carries IP68 and IP69 ratings simultaneously. IP68 means it can handle submersion in water. IP69 means it can withstand high-pressure, high-temperature water jets — a standard more typically associated with industrial equipment than consumer phones. For everyday waterproofing purposes, this is more than adequate. For peace of mind around rain, spills, and general life, it's excellent.

The battery is a 5,200mAh cell, paired with 68W wired fast charging. Motorola's European Edge 70 Fusion offers a 7,000mAh silicon-carbon variant in some markets, rated at up to 50 hours of battery life — but the Fusion+ currently ships with the 5,200mAh configuration, rated at up to 39 hours in normal usage. That's still a full day-plus for most users, and 68W charging means you're not waiting long when the battery does need topping up.

The phone measures 8mm thick, weighs between 177g and 193g depending on variant, and comes in Pantone Blue Surf and Orient Blue colorways. The quad-curved display and slim profile give it a feel that the price doesn't prepare you for.

## Who Should Buy This, and Who Should Skip It

The Fusion+ makes a strong case for anyone who wants a capable, well-built Android phone without paying flagship prices. If your daily use involves photography, streaming, social media, and general productivity, this phone handles all of it capably, adds a real telephoto lens, and delivers a display that competes with phones costing significantly more.

It is not for you if you need top-tier sustained gaming performance — the Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 is not that chip. It is not for you if you need the absolute best low-light camera quality, which still belongs to the Pixel 10 Pro and Galaxy S26 Ultra at the premium end. And it is not currently widely available — as of this writing, the Fusion+ is listed only in Brazil, with broader regional availability still unconfirmed.

That availability question is the Fusion+'s biggest practical limitation right now. A phone this capable deserves a wider launch, and it's hard to recommend hardware you can't easily buy. Motorola has suggested expansion to other South American markets, but global availability remains open.

## The Bigger Question This Phone Raises

There is a version of this conversation that goes beyond one phone review. The Motorola Edge 70 Fusion+ is the latest piece of evidence for something the mid-range smartphone market has been quietly demonstrating for a few years now: the gap between what you need and what flagships cost has never been wider.

A 144Hz AMOLED display, a triple-camera system with optical zoom, IP68/IP69 waterproofing, Android 16, five years of security support, 12GB of RAM, 256GB of storage, and 68W fast charging. In a phone that costs around $570. The honest question — the one this phone implicitly asks — is what exactly you are getting for $800, $1,000, or $1,200 more from a flagship that this phone doesn't already cover for most people's actual daily use.

The answer, if you dig into it, is usually some combination of the absolute best camera system available, the fastest chip available, premium materials and brand prestige, and features at the margins like satellite connectivity or stylus support. For some people, those things justify the price. For a lot of people, they don't. The Fusion+ is a well-timed reminder of that.

---

## Verdict

The Motorola Edge 70 Fusion+ is a thoughtfully constructed mid-range phone that delivers genuine value across the hardware areas that matter most. The display is exceptional, the camera system is versatile with its real telephoto lens, the software experience is clean, and the long-term update commitment is better than most competitors in this price range. The 5,200mAh battery and 68W charging keep daily use practical.

The limitations are real — availability is currently narrow, gaming performance has a ceiling, and camera quality requires real-world testing to fully evaluate against the spec sheet numbers. But as a package, the Fusion+ makes a compelling argument that most people's next phone doesn't need to cost $1,000.

Sometimes the mid-range is exactly where you should be.

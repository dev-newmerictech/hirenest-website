## Why the iPhone Fold Matters Right Now

Apple has been watching the foldable market from the sidelines for the better part of a decade. Samsung launched the original Galaxy Fold in 2019. Google, Motorola, Huawei, and a wave of Chinese manufacturers followed. Apple watched, refined, and waited. The conventional explanation is that Apple doesn't rush categories — it enters when it believes it can define them. The less charitable reading is that foldable phones spent several years being genuinely not good enough, and Apple was right to wait.

Both things are probably true. The first-generation foldables were fragile, expensive, and featured visible creases that made their flagship displays feel unfinished. Each successive generation from Samsung and Google improved on those problems incrementally. By 2025, foldable phones were genuinely excellent devices — better hinges, better displays, better durability — but the crease remained the stubborn unsolved problem that no manufacturer had fully conquered.

That changed at CES 2026, when Samsung Display showcased a new crease-less foldable OLED panel beside the Galaxy Z Fold 7. Multiple attendees and sources, including Bloomberg reporters, confirmed the panel showed virtually no crease compared to previous Samsung displays. Almost immediately, supply-chain reports emerged indicating that Apple had secured this same panel technology for the iPhone Fold. A crease-free display has reportedly been Apple's stated prerequisite for entering the foldable market — the thing it said it wouldn't do until it could do it right. With that final technical barrier apparently cleared, the timing crystallized.

The cumulative weight of leaks, supply-chain confirmations, analyst reports, and CAD renders now creates a picture that is unusually detailed for an unannounced Apple product. Ming-Chi Kuo, Mark Gurman, and Jeff Pu — three of the most reliable Apple analysts working today — have all confirmed second-half 2026 as Apple's target. Foxconn entered the New Product Introduction phase in early 2025, mass production is expected to begin in mid-2026, and Apple is reportedly already stockpiling components. The iPhone Fold is not a rumor in the usual sense. It is a product arriving later this year.

## The Display: Apple's Defining Technical Claim

The display is the centerpiece of the iPhone Fold's entire value proposition, and it's where Apple appears to have invested the most development effort. According to reports from MacRumors and Engadget, Apple pursued eliminating the crease "regardless of cost" and has developed "a new material property" that makes the crease disappear. The panel reportedly combines flexible OLED with a laser-drilled metal support plate that disperses stress when folding, the same general approach previewed in Samsung Display's CES 2026 demonstration.

**Why it matters:** Every current foldable phone has a crease. Some are subtle, most are visible under certain lighting, and all of them remind you that you're using a phone with a folded screen. If Apple genuinely delivers a crease-free inner display, it eliminates the most cited reason people buy a foldable and then return it. A crease-free standard isn't just a spec improvement — it's a category-defining capability that none of Apple's competitors currently offers.

**What the specs say:** The inner display measures approximately 7.7 to 7.8 inches with a resolution of 2,713 × 1,920 — slightly smaller than the iPad mini's 8.3-inch screen but close enough that iOS can deliver a genuine tablet-like experience when unfolded. The outer display sits at 5.49 inches at 2,088 × 1,422, designed to function like a conventional iPhone when the device is folded. Both displays are LTPO OLED, meaning the refresh rate adjusts dynamically based on content, preserving battery life.

**Limitation:** The inner display is smaller than Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 7 at 8.0 inches and Google's Pixel 9 Pro Fold at 8.0 inches. For users who want maximum screen real estate when unfolded, the iPhone Fold's slightly narrower canvas is a meaningful trade-off.

## The Chip: A20 Pro Across All Three Premium Models

Apple will equip the iPhone Fold with the same A20 Pro chip as the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max — a decision confirmed by analyst Jeff Pu. This is the right call. Foldable phones have historically suffered from thermal constraints that caused chips to throttle performance; the device simply couldn't cool itself efficiently given the thin profile and complex hinge mechanism.

**Why it matters:** Giving the iPhone Fold the same chip as the Pro lineup eliminates performance anxiety entirely. This is not a phone where Apple compromised on the processor to hit a price point. The A20 Pro will be the most powerful chip in any foldable smartphone when the iPhone Fold ships, and Apple's tight hardware-software integration means iOS will run exceptionally well on it.

**How it works:** The A20 Pro is expected to bring substantial improvements in neural engine performance — critical for Apple Intelligence features — alongside efficiency gains that should help offset the power demands of two displays. The device also reportedly includes a C2 chip for satellite connectivity, following the precedent set by recent iPhone Pro models.

## The Cameras: Four Sensors, Two Displays

The camera system on the iPhone Fold is shaped by an unusual constraint: the device needs to serve photographers in both folded and unfolded states, across both displays. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman confirmed four cameras total — one front camera for the outer display, one under-screen camera for the inner display, and two rear cameras.

**Why it matters:** The dual rear cameras — both reportedly 48MP — and the decision to use an under-display camera for the inner screen represent a deliberate design philosophy. Rather than compromising the inner display with a punch-hole cutout, Apple is placing the selfie camera beneath the display surface. Under-display camera technology has improved significantly since its early, blurry implementations, and Apple reportedly has an 18MP sensor in each position.

**Use cases:** Shooting photos when unfolded gives you the full inner screen as a viewfinder — a genuinely excellent experience that current foldable users cite as one of the best reasons to use a foldable camera. Shooting while folded behaves like a standard iPhone. Video calls use the inner display's under-screen camera automatically when the phone is fully open.

**Limitation:** The dual rear camera system means the iPhone Fold doesn't have a dedicated telephoto lens — a feature present on iPhone 18 Pro, Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7, and Google Pixel 9 Pro Fold. For users who rely on optical zoom, this is a real trade-off Apple has apparently chosen to accept in favor of an ultra-thin profile.

## The Hinge: Liquid Metal and a Five-Year Lifespan Target

The hinge is what makes or breaks a foldable phone over time. Apple's solution reportedly uses liquid metal — an amorphous alloy Apple has been developing for over 15 years — combined with improved titanium alloys. The design draws from a waterdrop hinge approach that allows the display to fold without sharp stress concentrations at the crease point.

**Why it matters:** A hinge that fails after 18 months of use would be catastrophic for Apple's reputation in the category. Apple is reportedly targeting durability standards equivalent to a five-year lifespan, a higher bar than Samsung's tested 200,000-fold rating for the Z Fold series. Whether that translates to real-world durability is something only time and independent testing will confirm.

**How it works:** The liquid metal hinge material's amorphous crystalline structure means it doesn't have the grain boundaries that cause conventional metals to crack under repeated stress. Combined with the titanium and aluminum hybrid frame confirmed by analyst Jeff Pu, the iPhone Fold is designed to feel premium in hand while maintaining structural integrity through years of daily folding and unfolding.

## The Price: Why It Will Shock You

Leaked pricing from a credible Weibo source, reported by MacRumors as recently as March 12, 2026, suggests the iPhone Fold will launch in three storage configurations: 256GB at approximately $2,320, 512GB at approximately $2,600, and 1TB at approximately $2,900.

Let that sink in. The base model is priced higher than the current top-tier Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7. The maxed-out configuration approaches $3,000. This is Apple's most expensive iPhone ever, by a substantial margin, and it prices the iPhone Fold out of the range of most casual upgrade buyers.

**Why the price is what it is:** The crease-free display alone — if it truly delivers — involves panel technology that costs more to manufacture than current foldable displays. The liquid metal hinge is more expensive to produce than standard titanium. Samsung reportedly negotiated a significantly higher price for the memory it supplies Apple for this device, due to tightening global memory supply. The A20 Pro chip costs more than its predecessors. Apple is not artificially inflating margins here; the component cost structure of what it's building is genuinely higher than anything it has shipped before.

**Who it's for:** The iPhone Fold is emphatically not positioned as a mainstream product. Ming-Chi Kuo projects 3 to 5 million units in 2026 and 20 million in 2027 — compare that to total iPhone shipments of roughly 200 million per year. This is a premium product for a specific buyer: someone who wants the most capable iOS device available, is willing to pay significantly for a crease-free foldable display, and doesn't need the telephoto lens the Pro and Pro Max offer.

## FAQ

**Is the iPhone Fold officially confirmed by Apple?**
No. Apple has not confirmed the iPhone Fold's existence. Everything known about the device comes from supply-chain leaks, analyst reports, and credible media sources including Bloomberg and MacRumors. Apple will not comment on unannounced products.

**Why does the iPhone Fold use Touch ID instead of Face ID?**
Multiple sources, including Ming-Chi Kuo and Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, indicate the iPhone Fold will use a Touch ID power button rather than Face ID. The reason is internal space — the complex hinge mechanism and dual displays leave insufficient room for Face ID's TrueDepth camera array. Touch ID is implemented via a side button, similar to iPad models.

**What storage options will the iPhone Fold come in?**
Leaked pricing from Weibo-based leaker Instant Digital suggests 256GB, 512GB, and 1TB configurations, starting at approximately $2,320 and topping out near $2,900. A base 512GB option, as noted by some sources, may serve as the practical entry point given the device's media and productivity use cases.

**Can the iPhone Fold slip to 2027?**
Yes, it remains a real possibility. Mizuho Securities has flagged a potential 2027 delay if Apple encounters manufacturing issues with the hinge or display. Apple has a history of delaying products it believes aren't ready. However, the weight of current supply-chain evidence — NPI completion, component stockpiling, mass production timeline alignment — points to a 2026 launch barring significant unforeseen problems.

**How does the inner display size compare to the iPad mini?**
The iPhone Fold's inner display measures approximately 7.7 to 7.8 inches, compared to the iPad mini's 8.3 inches. It's close enough that iOS can provide a genuine tablet-like multitasking experience when unfolded, but it's not a replacement for the iPad mini — it's smaller, thinner, and obviously more portable given that it folds into a conventional smartphone footprint.

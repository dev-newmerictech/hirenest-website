## Why Hiring a Janitor Is Harder Than It Looks

Most small business owners underestimate this hire. It feels simple — post a job, pick someone, done. But janitorial turnover consistently exceeds 100% annually at many businesses, meaning the average janitor stays less than a year.

The reason isn't pay alone. It's poor hiring — owners pick the first person who shows up without checking for reliability or fit. Then they're rehiring three months later, paying recruitment costs again, and dealing with inconsistent cleaning in between.

Getting this right the first time saves real money.

## Step 1: Define Exactly What You Need Before You Post

Before writing a single word of a job posting, answer these:

**Scope of work:** List every area to be cleaned — bathrooms, kitchen, office spaces, windows, floors, trash. Be specific.

**Frequency:** Daily, three times a week, or weekly? During business hours or after closing?

**Hours:** Part-time or full-time? Which days and times exactly?

**Equipment:** Do you provide supplies and equipment, or does the hire bring their own?

**Special requirements:** Biohazard cleanup, commercial kitchen cleaning, floor stripping, and waxing all require different skills and pay accordingly.

Getting clear on all this before posting means your job description attracts the right candidates — not just everyone who needs a job.

---

## Step 2: Write a Job Posting That Attracts Reliable Candidates

The biggest mistake in janitorial job postings is being too vague. "Looking for a cleaner — must be reliable" tells candidates nothing and attracts everyone.

A strong janitorial job posting includes:

**Specific duties:**
- Clean and sanitize restrooms daily
- Vacuum and mop all floor surfaces
- Empty trash and replace liners
- Clean kitchen and break room including appliances
- Restock paper products and soap dispensers
- Report maintenance issues to management

**Clear scheduling:** "Monday–Friday, 6pm–9pm" beats "evenings."

**What you provide:** Supplies, equipment, uniform, parking.

**Honest pay range:** Listings with a pay range receive 30% more applications than those without.

## Step 3: Where to Post the Job

For janitorial roles, these channels work best:

**Indeed:** Highest volume for hourly and service workers. A free listing with a small $5–$15/day sponsored boost gets fast applications.

**Facebook Jobs:** Highly effective for local hourly roles. Many janitorial candidates actively search here.

**Craigslist:** Still surprisingly effective for local service roles in most US markets.

**Nextdoor and local Facebook groups:** Reach candidates who want work close to home — a strong predictor of reliability.

**Word of mouth:** Referrals from trusted sources consistently produce better hires in service roles than any job board.

## Step 4: How to Screen Applications Quickly

Use this fast framework:

**Immediate disqualifiers:**
- No response to specific questions in your posting
- Generic copy-paste applications
- Requested hours or pay dramatically outside what you posted

**Green flags:**
- Specific janitorial or cleaning experience listed
- Consistent employment history
- Response that addresses your specific requirements
- Local address — proximity reduces no-shows

Don't over-filter on resume quality. Many excellent janitors are not strong resume writers. A short phone call reveals more in this role.

## Step 5: The 10-Minute Phone Screen

Before a full interview, a brief phone screen filters out poor fits fast.

**Phone screen questions:**
- "Can you confirm you're available [specific days and hours]?"
- "Tell me about your most recent cleaning role."
- "Do you have reliable transportation to get to [location] by [time]?"
- "Are you comfortable working independently without supervision?"
- "When could you start?"

If they answer confidently and match your requirements, move to an in-person interview.

---

## Step 6: The In-Person Interview

For janitorial roles, the in-person interview serves two purposes: assessing fit and confirming they actually show up when they say they will.

A candidate who arrives on time, dressed appropriately, and ready to discuss their experience is already demonstrating the core qualities you need.

**Key interview questions:**

*"Walk me through how you would clean a commercial bathroom from start to finish."*
Reveals whether they know the job or are improvising.

*"Tell me about a time you noticed something broken or needing repair. What did you do?"*
Tests initiative and communication for a solo worker.

*"What cleaning products and equipment have you used? Any experience with industrial floor cleaners or commercial disinfectants?"*
Assesses real experience level.

*"Describe the most physically demanding cleaning job you've had and how you managed it."*
Janitorial work is physical — you want realistic self-awareness.

*"If you finished your tasks early, what would you do?"*
Reveals work ethic and attitude toward the role.

## Step 7: Always Check References

Reference checks are skipped more than any other step in janitorial hiring — and it is a costly mistake. A 5-minute call to a previous employer reveals:

- Whether they showed up consistently
- Whether they needed constant supervision
- Whether they were trustworthy working alone
- Whether the employer would rehire them

The single most useful question: *"Would you rehire this person if you had the opportunity?"* The hesitation tells you as much as the words.

---

## Step 8: Make a Clear Written Offer

Once you have your candidate, put the offer in writing. Include:

- Start date and hours
- Pay rate and pay schedule
- Probationary period (30–90 days is standard)
- What happens at probation end
- Equipment or uniform they will receive

A clear written offer reduces the misunderstandings that cause early departures.

---

## Step 9: The First Week Sets Everything

Most janitorial hires that fail do so in the first two weeks — not because they cannot do the job, but because expectations were unclear.

On day one:
- Walk through every area and show your standard
- Demonstrate how you want things done, not just what needs doing
- Give them a checklist they can reference independently
- Introduce them to anyone else who works in the space

Check in at the end of week one. Address any gaps immediately rather than waiting.

## FAQ

**How much should I pay a janitor for a small business in 2026?**
Fair market rates range from $15–$20/hour in most US markets. In high cost-of-living cities, expect $18–$25/hour. Paying at or above market rate significantly reduces turnover.

**Should I hire an independent janitor or a cleaning company?**
An independent janitor costs less per hour but requires more management from you. A cleaning company charges more but handles scheduling, backup coverage, and supplies. For straightforward cleaning needs, an independent hire is more cost-effective if managed well.

**Do I need to run a background check on a janitor?**
Yes, especially if they will work alone in your facility after hours or have access to secure areas. A basic criminal background check ($15–$30) is worth it for any role with unsupervised facility access.

**How long does it take to hire a janitor?**
With an active posting and prompt follow-up, most small businesses can hire a janitor within 1–2 weeks. Slow responses to applications extend this significantly.

**What if my janitor quits suddenly?**
Build a backup plan before you need it. Keep the contact of your second-choice candidate from the last hiring round, and maintain a relationship with a local cleaning company who can provide emergency coverage while you rehire.

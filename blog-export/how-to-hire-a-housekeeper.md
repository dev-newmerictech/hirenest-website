## Why Hiring a Housekeeper Requires More Care Than Most Hires

A housekeeper is not just an employee — they are someone you are inviting into your private space, often when you are not there. They will have access to your home, your belongings, and potentially your family members.

This makes the hiring decision more personal and higher-stakes than most. The wrong hire is not just an inconvenience — it can mean theft, property damage, or a breach of your family's privacy.

Taking the hiring process seriously from the start is not paranoia. It is good judgment.

---

## Step 1: Define What You Actually Need

Before posting a job or contacting an agency, get specific about your requirements:

**Type of cleaning:** Routine maintenance cleaning (dusting, vacuuming, mopping, bathrooms) vs. deep cleaning vs. both? Are there specialty tasks like laundry, ironing, or organizing?

**Frequency:** Once a week, twice a week, daily, or live-in?

**Hours:** How many hours per visit do you estimate? Being realistic here avoids friction later.

**Additional duties:** Some housekeepers also cook, run errands, or help with childcare. Be clear upfront about whether you expect these — many housekeepers will decline a role where extra duties were added after hiring.

**Property specifics:** Large home, multiple floors, pets, children, delicate surfaces or antiques all affect the complexity of the role and the pay expectation.

---

## Step 2: Where to Find Housekeeper Candidates

**Household staffing agencies:** The most reliable source for vetted candidates. Agencies pre-screen, check references, and verify backgrounds. They charge a placement fee (typically one to two weeks of pay) but the vetting saves significant time.

**Referrals from friends and neighbors:** The single best source if available. A housekeeper trusted by someone you know personally comes with a built-in reference.

**Care.com and similar platforms:** Large databases of household workers with reviews. Quality varies widely — treat it as a starting point, not a finished solution.

**Indeed and Facebook Jobs:** Good for local reach, particularly for part-time cleaning roles.

**Local community boards and neighborhood apps:** Nextdoor and local Facebook groups often surface local candidates who work in your neighborhood already.

---

## Step 3: The Interview — What to Ask

Never skip a structured interview, even for a referral. Here are the questions that matter most:

**Experience and skills:**
- "What types of homes have you cleaned — size, number of floors, special surfaces?"
- "Do you have experience with [specific surfaces in your home — marble, hardwood, antiques]?"
- "What cleaning products do you typically use, and are you open to using what I provide?"
- "Have you ever handled laundry and ironing as part of your role?"

**Reliability and character:**
- "Tell me about your most recent housekeeping role. Why did it end?"
- "Have you ever had a misunderstanding with a client about your duties? How did you handle it?"
- "What would you do if you accidentally broke something while cleaning?"
- "If you were running late or needed to miss a scheduled day, what would you do?"

**Practical logistics:**
- "Do you have reliable transportation?"
- "Are you comfortable being in the home alone when the family is not there?"
- "Do you have any scheduling conflicts on [specific days]?"

---

## Step 4: The Most Important Red Flags

These signals during the hiring process should give you serious pause:

**Vague or unavailable references:** A housekeeper with years of experience should be able to provide 2–3 verifiable references from previous clients. Reluctance to provide references or references who cannot be reached is a significant warning sign.

**Inconsistent or unexplained work history gaps:** Short tenures are common in household staffing, but multiple positions ending abruptly without clear explanation warrants direct follow-up.

**Reluctance to undergo a background check:** Any qualified candidate for a role with home access should be willing to consent to a background check. Resistance is a red flag regardless of the stated reason.

**Bringing unauthorized people:** Some housekeepers bring helpers, family members, or friends without disclosure. Ask directly upfront: "Will it ever be anyone other than you doing the cleaning?"

**Reluctance to discuss specific cleaning methods:** Someone who genuinely has housekeeping experience can talk in detail about their process. Vague or generic answers to practical questions suggest limited real experience.

**Asking for cash only with no paper trail:** While cash payment is common in household staffing, insisting on it with no documentation whatsoever can indicate an unwillingness to be accountable.

---

## Step 5: Reference Checks Are Non-Negotiable

Call every reference. Ask:

- "How long did [name] work for you?"
- "What were their primary duties?"
- "Was there ever a time something went missing or was damaged while they worked for you?"
- "Did they show up consistently and on time?"
- "Would you hire them again?"

The last question is the most revealing. A genuine positive reference will answer quickly and enthusiastically. Hesitation, excessive qualifiers, or "I'd rather not say" are all signals worth paying attention to.

---

## Step 6: Run a Background Check

For anyone who will be in your home unsupervised, a background check is essential. Services like Checkr, Sterling, or even the screening built into Care.com provide basic criminal history checks for $20–$40.

What to look for: theft-related offenses, fraud, or violent crime history. Minor traffic violations or old misdemeanors are generally not disqualifying, but theft-related history in a home-access role is a serious concern.

---

## Step 7: Start With a Trial Clean

Before committing to a regular arrangement, pay for a single trial cleaning session. This lets you:

- Evaluate the quality of their work against your standards
- See how they handle your space and belongings
- Check whether they ask appropriate questions or just make assumptions
- Assess punctuality and professionalism

Give them a full walkthrough before they start, then evaluate the results honestly. A genuinely good housekeeper will welcome the trial — they want to make sure it's a good fit too.

---

## Step 8: Set Clear Expectations in Writing

Once you have made your decision, put the arrangement in writing. This does not need to be a formal legal contract — a simple one-page document covering:

- Scheduled days and hours
- List of duties to be completed each visit
- Pay rate and payment method
- Notice required from either side to end the arrangement
- Any house rules (security codes, pet handling, areas that are off-limits)

This document protects both parties and eliminates the ambiguity that causes most household employment disputes.

## FAQ

**How much should I pay a housekeeper in 2026?**
For private household cleaning in the US, fair rates in 2026 range from $18–$28/hour depending on location and scope. In major metro areas, expect to pay toward the higher end. Live-in housekeepers typically earn $35,000–$55,000 annually with housing included.

**Should I use an agency or hire independently?**
Agencies cost more but pre-screen candidates and handle some HR complexities. Independent hires cost less but require more due diligence from you. For a first-time household employer or a complex home with significant responsibility, an agency is often worth the premium.

**Is it legal to pay a housekeeper cash?**
Cash payment is legal, but if a housekeeper works regularly in your home, you may be required by law to withhold payroll taxes and issue tax documentation depending on hours and pay. Consult a tax professional — household employment tax rules ("nanny tax" rules) apply to housekeepers who meet the threshold.

**What if my housekeeper damages something?**
This is worth discussing before hiring begins. Professionals in this field expect occasional accidents and will tell you upfront how they handle them. Ask directly in the interview what they would do if something was broken. A good housekeeper will say they would tell you immediately — not hope you don't notice.

**How do I let a housekeeper go if it's not working out?**
Be direct and respectful. Pay any owed wages, give reasonable notice if the arrangement has been ongoing, and keep the conversation professional. If you have a written agreement with a notice clause, honor it. Most housekeepers are independent contractors or employees-at-will, but treating people fairly matters regardless of the legal minimum.

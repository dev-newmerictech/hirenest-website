**TL;DR (Direct Answer):** A structured interview is an evaluation methodology where every candidate for a specific role is asked the exact same set of predefined questions and graded on the exact same 1-5 scoring rubric. Unlike an unstructured interview where managers ask whatever comes to mind, structured interviews (like those generated by Hirenest) neutralize bias and double the predictive validity of the hiring process.

## 1. The Structured Interview Definition

To master modern hiring, you must understand the **structured interview definition**. A structured interview requires rigid calibration. 

The core requirements are:
1. **Identical Prompts:** Candidate A and Candidate B are asked the exact same behavioral and situational questions. 
2. **Pre-defined Intent:** The interviewer knows *exactly* what skill a question is testing before they ask it.
3. **Behavioral Anchors:** The interviewer does not grade on a generic "Thumbs Up/Thumbs Down." They grade using a 1-5 rubric equipped with specific situational anchors.

## 2. Unstructured vs Structured Interviews

The debate of **unstructured vs structured interviews** is considered settled in the world of industrial-organizational psychology.

| Feature | Structured Interview (Hirenest Method) | Unstructured Interview ("Gut Feeling") |

## 3. Structured Interview Examples

Deploying this format is easy when you use the right technology. Here is a classic example of transitioning from bad interviewing to structured interviewing using the STAR+ framework.

### The Problem: The Unstructured Query
*Bad Question:* "How do you handle stress?"
*Why it fails:* Candidates will give a generic, rehearsed answer ("I do yoga!"). You have learned nothing about their actual work output.

### The Solution: The Structured Alternative
*Structured Question (Generated by Hirenest):* "Tell me about a time you were given a project with an impossible deadline and conflicting stakeholder demands. How did you prioritize, and what was the outcome?"

**The Structured 1-5 Scoring Rubric for this Question:**
*   **5 (Exceptional):** Candidate immediately flagged the impossible deadline, pushed back using data, negotiated a phased rollout, and delivered Phase 1 successfully.
*   **3 (Adequate):** Candidate worked overtime to finish it, succeeding, but failed to communicate the risk to stakeholders or manage expectations.
*   **1 (Poor):** Candidate struggled, missed the deadline entirely, and blamed other departments.

## The Nuclear Pivot: Why SMRs?

Project Olympus isn't building the massive, dome-shaped nuclear plants of the 1970s. They are betting on **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)**. 

SMRs are smaller, safer, and can be manufactured in a factory and shipped to the site. This allows Amazon to scale their power capacity incrementally. If they need another 500 megawatts to train "Olympus-2," they simply plug in another SMR module. 

This move also serves as a massive **greenwashing hedge**. By using carbon-free nuclear energy, Amazon can technically meet its "Net Zero" climate pledges while simultaneously consuming more electricity than the city of Chicago.

## FAQ

**Is a nuclear-powered data center safe?**
Modern SMR designs are "passively safe," meaning they don't require human intervention or electricity to shut down in an emergency. Amazon is also placing these facilities in remote, geologically stable areas (rumored to be in the Pacific Northwest and the Appalachian regions) to minimize any risk to population centers.

**Will this make my Amazon deliveries faster?**
In the long run, yes. The AI trained on Project Olympus is being designed to manage Amazon’s global logistics—predicting what you want to buy before you buy it and autonomously routing millions of delivery drones and robots.

**Why is everyone suddenly obsessed with nuclear energy for AI?**
Because AI is "always-on" compute. Solar and wind are great, but they are intermittent. You can't stop a training run just because the sun went down. Nuclear is the only carbon-free energy source that provides "baseload" power—constant, high-intensity electricity 24 hours a day.

**Is Jeff Bezos involved in this?**
While Bezos has stepped back from daily operations, Project Olympus aligns perfectly with his long-term vision of moving heavy industry (and now heavy compute) off the standard grid and eventually into space. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy is the primary architect of the Olympus pivot.
